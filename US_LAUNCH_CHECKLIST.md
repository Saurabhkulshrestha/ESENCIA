# ESEN’CIA — U.S. launch checklist (India → United States)

This is a planning checklist, not legal, tax, customs, or regulatory advice. Confirm the details for your state, product formulas, labels, and shipment with qualified U.S. professionals before selling or importing inventory.

## 1. Confirm the products and manufacturer
- Obtain the final formula and complete ingredient declaration for each SKU using U.S. cosmetic ingredient names/INCI and verify ingredient identity, concentration, and intended use.
- Obtain current safety substantiation for each finished product, including toxicological review and relevant stability, microbiological/preservative efficacy, and packaging-compatibility testing as appropriate to the formula.
- Get batch/lot traceability, manufacturing records, certificates of analysis where applicable, specifications, shelf life/period-after-opening rationale, complaint handling, adverse-event escalation, and recall procedures.
- Confirm in writing who owns the ESEN’CIA brand, formula, packaging artwork, and U.S. distribution rights. Agree on quality standards, minimum order quantities, lead times, Incoterms, defective goods, and recalls.
- Ask the Indian manufacturer whether the manufacturing facility has an FDA Establishment Identifier (FEI) and whether required FDA cosmetic facility registration is current. Verify directly; FDA registration/listing is not an FDA approval or endorsement.

## 2. U.S. cosmetic compliance before sale
- Check every formula against U.S. restrictions and requirements for ingredients, color additives, and intended use.
- Decide who is the U.S. “responsible person” whose name appears on the label. Under MoCRA, the responsible person generally lists each marketed cosmetic product with FDA and updates listings annually, unless an applicable exemption applies. The manufacturing/processing facility generally must register and renew every two years unless exempt.
- Evaluate whether the small-business exemption applies; do not assume it applies without checking statutory criteria and exceptions.
- Maintain adequate safety substantiation and required records. Set up a process for serious adverse-event reporting and recordkeeping under MoCRA.
- Keep marketing claims cosmetic in nature. Avoid disease-treatment claims (for example, treating acne/eczema/rosacea) or claims that affect body structure/function unless specialist regulatory counsel confirms the product’s regulatory category and requirements. Claims such as “clinically proven” or quantified results need appropriate evidence for the exact formula and claim.

## 3. U.S. label review
Have a U.S. cosmetics regulatory specialist review each final label and online product page before printing/sale. Typical label elements include:
- Product identity/use statement and net quantity in U.S. customary units (metric may also be shown).
- Ingredient declaration using accepted U.S. common/usual names, typically in descending order as required.
- Name and place of business of the responsible firm; include appropriate “Manufactured for” or “Distributed by” wording if applicable.
- Country of origin for imported goods (e.g., “Made in India”) in the required format/location.
- Required warnings/directions and any applicable contact details.
- Legible, truthful, non-misleading claims; confirm both immediate container and outer packaging requirements.

## 4. U.S. business setup
- Choose the state where you will operate and compare sole proprietorship vs. LLC vs. corporation with a CPA/attorney. Many small operators consider an LLC, but it is not automatically best for every owner or tax situation.
- Register the entity/name with the state as required; obtain an EIN directly from the IRS when applicable; open a dedicated business bank account and bookkeeping system.
- Check state/local business licenses, home-business/zoning rules if relevant, and sales-tax registration/nexus obligations for every state where you sell. Online marketplaces may collect some sales tax, but that does not automatically remove all seller obligations.
- Obtain product liability insurance that expressly covers imported skincare/cosmetics and U.S. sales; consider general liability, cargo/transit, and cyber coverage as relevant.
- Review trademark availability and consider protecting ESEN’CIA and product/complex names in the U.S. before investing heavily in marketing.

## 5. Importing from India
- Decide who will be importer of record and who will act as customs broker. The importer remains responsible for accurate entry information and compliance even when using a broker.
- Ask a licensed customs broker to classify each product under the correct HTSUS code, confirm current duty/tariff treatment for India, and estimate duty, fees, and any additional trade measures before placing a purchase order. Do not rely on an unverified duty estimate.
- Prepare commercial invoice, packing list, transport document, country-of-origin details, product/ingredient information requested by FDA/CBP, and any required prior entry data. Make sure product names, values, quantities, manufacturer and origin are consistent across documents.
- Confirm shipment terms (Incoterms), freight forwarder, cargo insurance, customs bond requirements, port/entry process, storage/demurrage responsibility, and what happens if FDA/CBP detains or refuses a shipment.
- Ensure the labels and product compliance are ready before shipment; a shipment can be delayed, detained, refused, or require corrective action if the entry or products are noncompliant.

## 6. Website and selling readiness
- The five serum products on this prototype are set to $12 each. Treat this as the planned retail price; it does not establish profitability. Calculate landed cost per unit, packaging, freight, duty, brokerage, storage, fulfillment, payment fees, returns, marketing, and taxes.
- Before accepting real orders, connect a payment processor, set shipping zones/rates, delivery estimates, returns/refund policy, privacy policy, terms, tax handling, customer support contact, order emails, and secure checkout.
- Test mobile pages, product photos, accessibility, cart quantity/subtotal, shipping/tax calculations, order confirmation, refunds, and inventory/lot traceability.
- The included checkout is a demo and does not process real payments. Do not use it to accept customer orders until a secure payment/fulfillment workflow is implemented and tested.
- Publish only claims, ingredient lists, product sizes, directions, and imagery that match the final sellable SKU and approved label.

## Suggested order of operations
1. Confirm launch state, business structure, and brand/distribution rights.
2. Collect final formulas, complete INCI lists, safety/testing records, manufacturing and lot traceability documents.
3. Have a U.S. cosmetic regulatory specialist review formulas, claims, labels, MoCRA roles/exemptions, and listing/registration requirements.
4. Have a customs broker confirm HTS classification, duties/tariffs, entry requirements, and shipment economics.
5. Register the business/tax accounts, obtain insurance, and set up bookkeeping.
6. Finalize labels and packaging, then import a small compliant test shipment.
7. Finish payment, shipping, returns, privacy, tax, customer support, and fulfillment setup before opening sales.
